The Boy Aflame Who Couldn't Fly

(Role: Gameplay Programmer)

The Boy Aflame Who Couldn't Fly is a narrative exploration game where the player records family events on a camcorder and replays footage across the years in Cuba. From a technical perspective, it focuses on a modular "recording + playback" framework where each object defines its own state serialization, replayed at a fixed timestep with interpolation for smooth playback.

What I contributed

  • Implemented the player controller and camera system, including both first-person and third-person perspectives with runtime swapping and camera smoothing.
  • Integrated Unity's New Input System package to support clean action mapping and consistent input handling across gameplay and UI.
  • Built the recording/playback "video" system, centered around a Replay Manager that captures object state at a fixed timestep and replays it with interpolation.
  • Designed the replay framework so each recordable object defines what attributes it needs, such as position, rotation, and velocity by default, reducing redundant recording and keeping captures lightweight.
  • Created UI systems for recording and playback, including the recording overlay and playback tooling.
  • Imported and integrated animations and 3D models into the project pipeline.

Teamwork and process

I worked on a studio team that collaborated primarily asynchronously through Discord. I focused on building core gameplay systems early, especially the player controller and the recording/playback framework that the project's central mechanic depends on.

Early on, I authored a game mechanics document for the whole team to align on how recording, playback, and player interaction should behave. I tracked my work and priorities in a personal Notion board to stay organized and keep momentum during the prototype sprint.

The core "video" mechanic required systems thinking: define state ownership per object, capture deterministically, then replay smoothly through interpolation.

What I learned

  • Replay systems get dramatically simpler when ownership is pushed to the objects being recorded, with each object defining what state matters.
  • Fixed-timestep capture paired with interpolation is a strong baseline for smooth playback without over-sampling state.
  • For short prototype sprints, producing a clear mechanics document early helps keep implementation decisions consistent across the team.
